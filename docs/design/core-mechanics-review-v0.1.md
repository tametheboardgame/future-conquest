# Core mechanics review v0.1

Status: Approved foundation

## Purpose

This checkpoint incorporates the accepted decisions arising from the first
logistics simulation before formations and combat are defined.

## Territory control

A territory counts towards victory only when all of the following are true:

- the strategic centre is controlled;
- organised defenders have surrendered, withdrawn or been suppressed;
- a functioning supply arrangement reaches the territory;
- authority is maintained by future troops, loyal modern forces, police or an
  accepted client government.

Entering a territory or winning one battle is not sufficient. Control can be
lost without a conventional counter-invasion if supply collapses, the client
government falls or the garrison defects.

## Modern forces

Modern forces are essential on Veteran and Desperate modes. They are highly
valuable but not strictly mandatory when the player begins with 50,000 or
100,000 future troops.

Modern forces can eventually replace up to 90 per cent of routine occupation
duties. Their availability and reliability depend on treatment, legitimacy,
nationality, casualties, supply, command relationships and global escalation.
They may refuse offensive operations, mutiny or defect.

The mechanical purpose is to free irreplaceable future troops for decisive
operations while creating political and loyalty risks in the rear.

## Captured supply

Captured territory provides limited emergency support immediately. Normal
capacity takes approximately seven turns to establish and may take longer when
infrastructure is damaged, resistance is active or civilian shortages are
severe.

Civilian demand competes with the military for transport and supply capacity.
The player may requisition a greater share, but doing so increases unrest,
resistance and escalation.

Supply loses effective capacity across long routes. Secure hubs, ports, rail
connections and controlled crossings reduce this loss. A large aggregate supply
total is therefore insufficient if it cannot reach the active formations.

## Campaign-duration targets

- 100,000 troops: approximately 60 to 100 turns.
- 50,000 troops: approximately 90 to 150 turns.
- 25,000 troops: approximately 140 to 220 turns.
- 10,000 troops: approximately 220 to 365 turns.

Quiet turns should resolve quickly. Major battles, diplomatic decisions and
occupation crises receive more player attention, so turn count is not a direct
measure of real-world playing time.

## Difficulty identity

- Campaign, 100,000: operational freedom and tolerance for mistakes.
- Standard, 50,000: meaningful division between fronts and garrisons.
- Veteran, 25,000: modern-force integration becomes compulsory.
- Desperate, 10,000: deliberate pauses, indirect control and political leverage
  are central to survival.

## Armour depletion

Routine operation causes gradual wear. Combat, inadequate maintenance,
environmental exposure and low supply accelerate it. The combat model must
track both casualties and the migration of surviving troops from fully armoured
to damaged-armour and finally unarmoured states.

## Combat-model requirements

The next package must test these approved mechanics rather than redefine them.
It must include formation scale, readiness, resistance bands, casualties,
armour damage, modern-force loyalty, operational delay and escalation pressure.
