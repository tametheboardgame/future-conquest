# Phase 2: European Campaign Map Design

Version: 0.1 research draft  
Date: 1 August 2026

## 1. Design decision

The canonical map will be the Standard Campaign map, containing approximately 100 playable land territories. The proposed first pass contains 101 territories. Rapid Campaign territories will be generated by merging Standard territories. Grand Campaign territories will be generated by splitting them, using the same source geography and identifiers.

The map is not intended to reproduce a single administrative classification. It will use recognisable administrative boundaries where possible, but strategic geography takes priority. A territory must be legible, selectable and militarily meaningful.

## 2. Geographic scope

The playable theatre includes:

- Iceland, Ireland, the United Kingdom and mainland continental Europe.
- The Nordic countries and Baltic states.
- Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova and the European portion of Russia.
- The Balkans, Greece, Malta and Cyprus.
- European Türkiye (Thrace), with Anatolia treated as an external reinforcement theatre.
- Kaliningrad as a separate disconnected territory.

The following are outside the conquest requirement:

- Greenland, Svalbard and remote European overseas territories.
- The Caucasus states and Asian Russia.
- Anatolia beyond the Bosporus.
- North Africa and the Middle East.

These off-map areas still exist. They can provide reinforcements, refugees, trade, diplomacy, air operations and foreign intervention.

Russia presents a special design case. The player is required to control its European strategic heartland rather than physically occupy the entire Russian Federation. Doing so can trigger capitulation, fragmentation, relocation of the Russian government or continued off-map resistance, depending on the World State and campaign events.

## 3. Political treatment

Territory geometry must be stored separately from political ownership and military control.

Each World State assigns:

- recognised claimant;
- actual controller;
- active conflict status;
- alliance membership;
- foreign deployments at an abstract level;
- mobilisation and reinforcement rules.

This permits the same map geometry to represent changing circumstances without redrawing the board. Crimea, Kosovo, Transnistria and other disputed or contested areas must be handled through explicit scenario data and neutral wording, with a clear in-game disclaimer that the map is a gameplay model rather than a statement on sovereignty.

Microstates are represented as political nodes rather than full military territories: Andorra, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City. Luxembourg remains a standard territory because it sits on important land routes and is large enough to display. Microstates can submit, resist diplomatically, host intelligence activity or create special events, but do not require a separate conventional campaign.

## 5. Territory construction rules

A candidate territory should satisfy most of the following:

- It has a recognisable regional identity.
- It contains at least one useful administrative or logistics centre.
- It produces meaningful movement choices.
- Its borders follow visible geography or established administrative lines.
- It is large enough to select at desktop and tablet scales.
- It does not create a long, thin or fragmented polygon without a strategic reason.
- It does not give a major state either excessive granularity or implausible simplicity.
- Its expected garrison and resource burden are materially different from adjacent territories.

Territories need not be equally sized or equally valuable. Balance comes through infrastructure, population, terrain, military deployment and occupation cost rather than equal land area.

## 6. Strategic nodes

Territories contain nodes rather than treating every point of interest as a separate board space.

Node types:

- national capital;
- regional administrative centre;
- major city;
- port;
- airport or airbase abstraction;
- rail and road hub;
- bridge or tunnel;
- energy site;
- logistics depot;
- future-army recharge site;
- nuclear command or storage abstraction;
- microstate;
- disputed or insurgent enclave.

The Standard map should initially target approximately:

- 101 territories;
- 150 to 220 major city and administrative nodes;
- 40 to 60 strategically significant ports;
- 35 to 50 major airports or air-operation nodes;
- 25 to 40 chokepoints and crossings;
- a deliberately abstract set of military deployment nodes generated by each World State.

Exact military sites should not be copied into the playable model. Public sources may inform broad regional capability, but starting forces should be generated within military regions to improve accuracy, safety and replayability.

## 7. Movement network

Territory adjacency alone is insufficient. Each connection should be an explicit route edge with properties.

Route types:

- open land border;
- mountain crossing;
- major bridge;
- tunnel;
- ferry route;
- short sea crossing;
- long sea route;
- airlift route;
- rail corridor;
- restricted or seasonal route.

Each edge can store:

- distance and movement cost;
- terrain;
- road and rail quality;
- daily capacity;
- fuel requirement;
- weather sensitivity;
- whether heavy vehicles can use it;
- whether it can be blocked, mined, bombed or demolished;
- repair requirements;
- controlling nodes.

The major European transport corridors should be derived from the EU's TEN-T network and then simplified into gameplay routes. The game should never attempt road-level pathfinding across the entire continent.

## 8. Essential chokepoints

The first map must deliberately represent:

- English Channel and Channel Tunnel;
- Strait of Dover ferry routes;
- Gibraltar and the western Mediterranean entrance;
- Pyrenean crossings;
- Alpine passes and tunnels;
- Rhine and Danube crossing capacity;
- Danish Straits and Øresund links;
- Baltic access and island positions;
- Suwałki corridor between Poland and Lithuania;
- Bosporus and Dardanelles;
- Adriatic coastal routes;
- Carpathian crossings;
- Brenner corridor;
- Gotthard corridor;
- Strait of Messina;
- key crossings of the Dnipro;
- Black Sea ports;
- access routes into Kaliningrad.

Chokepoints should be destructible or blockable, but destruction must also damage the player's future logistics and occupation economy.

## 9. Seas and islands

The player begins without naval assets, so bodies of water must matter without creating a full naval-warfare simulator.

The map will contain abstract sea zones:

- North Atlantic;
- Norwegian Sea;
- North Sea;
- English Channel;
- Baltic Sea;
- Bay of Biscay;
- Western Mediterranean;
- Central Mediterranean;
- Adriatic Sea;
- Aegean Sea;
- Black Sea.

Sea movement requires controlled port capacity and acquired shipping. Naval and air superiority modify interception risk. Small formations may use civilian vessels, while large formations require ferries, roll-on/roll-off ships or sustained sealift.

Island territories must therefore be strategically difficult but not campaign-ending when selected as portal starts. A portal opening on an island should guarantee enough immediately accessible transport opportunity to make escape possible, though not necessarily easy.

## 10. Terrain and weather

Terrain will be calculated during map production and stored as simplified territory and route attributes. It will not be sampled live during gameplay.

Primary terrain classes:

- urbanised;
- open lowland;
- forest;
- hills;
- mountain;
- wetland or river delta;
- Mediterranean dry terrain;
- northern or arctic terrain;
- major river barrier;
- island or coastal.

Terrain affects movement, detection, air attack exposure, supply, defensive strength and armour degradation. Weather should operate regionally and seasonally rather than simulate individual clouds. Snow, extreme heat, storms, flooding and mud can alter route capacity and energy consumption.

## 11. Portal placement

Portal arrival is random but weighted and validated.

The selection process should:

1. Select an eligible Standard territory.
2. Select a valid land arrival zone within it.
3. Exclude water, sheer mountain terrain, tiny islands, dense city centres and areas too close to the map edge.
4. Guarantee minimum space for the chosen force size.
5. Measure immediate access to food, water, roads and potential transport.
6. Assign a start-difficulty rating.
7. Generate nearby military, police and civilian reactions from the active World State.

Players can choose Fully Random, Balanced Random, Favourable Start or Hostile Start. Daily and seeded campaigns can reuse a published portal seed.

## 12. Territorial control

A territory has one of the following states:

- sovereign control;
- mobilising;
- invaded;
- contested;
- occupied;
- client-controlled;
- insurgent;
- collapsed;
- abandoned or inaccessible.

Occupation is not complete merely because a future formation crosses the border. Control requires:

- control of the administrative centre or equivalent command node;
- no organised hostile force capable of contesting the territory;
- a functioning supply connection or local supply base;
- sufficient garrison, allied authority or negotiated compliance;
- an acceptable resistance level.

The control overlay should visually distinguish conquest, secure control, contest and client-state control.

## 13. Recommended geographic data stack

### Base geography

- Natural Earth 1:10m or 1:50m for coastlines, oceans and a clean public-domain world backdrop.
- Eurostat GISCO NUTS 2024 and statistical regions for European regional boundaries.
- Natural Earth Admin-1 and EuroGlobalMap to fill gaps outside NUTS coverage.
- Hand-authored merge and split definitions for the final 101 gameplay territories.

### Population and settlements

- Eurostat GISCO population grids and Census 2021 layers for the EU and participating states.
- GISCO and EuroGlobalMap settlements for major centres.
- Country-level contemporary population data in the World State, with regional weights precomputed from geographic sources.

### Transport

- European Commission TEN-T corridors for strategic road, rail, port and urban-node selection.
- GISCO port and airport datasets.
- EuroGlobalMap transport and hydrography for cross-checking and non-EU coverage.
- OpenStreetMap only where additional route validation is necessary, with ODbL attribution and derived-database obligations reviewed before inclusion.

### Terrain

- Copernicus DEM GLO-30 or EU-DEM for elevation-derived terrain.
- European Environment Agency biogeographical regions for broad environmental classification.
- Natural Earth rivers and lakes for visual and strategic barriers.

## 14. Data licensing approach

The safest core is public-domain Natural Earth plus properly attributed European Commission and EuroGeographics open data. Every imported dataset must have a source record containing:

- title;
- publisher;
- version and retrieval date;
- source URL;
- licence;
- required attribution;
- transformations applied;
- files or game fields derived from it.

OpenStreetMap data is usable under the ODbL, including commercially, but it creates attribution and database-sharing considerations. It should not become the unexamined foundation of the game. If used, it should be isolated to a documented source layer and attributed visibly.

## 15. Map production pipeline

The planned production process is:

1. Acquire and archive source datasets with licences and versions.
2. Convert sources to a common coordinate system.
3. Build a canonical regional source layer.
4. Apply authored merge and split rules to create the 101 territory polygons.
5. Validate topology, removing gaps, overlaps and invalid polygons.
6. Create stable territory identifiers that never depend on display names.
7. Build an explicit territory adjacency and transport-edge graph.
8. Add cities, ports, airports, crossings and political nodes.
9. Calculate terrain, population and infrastructure summaries offline.
10. Simplify geometry for browser performance.
11. Export TopoJSON for rendering and compact JSON for simulation.
12. Run automated tests for topology, reachability, isolated territories and required metadata.
13. Visually review the complete map at desktop and tablet sizes.

No live GIS processing should be required in the player's browser.

## 16. Derived campaign modes

### Rapid Campaign

- Approximately 45 to 60 territories.
- Merge neighbouring Standard territories using a fixed parent identifier.
- Retain capitals, major ports and decisive chokepoints.
- Reduce occupation administration without changing the basic military model.

### Standard Campaign

- 101 proposed territories.
- Primary balancing and narrative mode.
- All systems designed against this map first.

### Grand Campaign

- Approximately 170 to 220 territories.
- Split Standard territories using NUTS 2, NUTS 3 or equivalent regional boundaries.
- Add secondary ports, airfields, crossings and cities.
- Increase logistical and occupation detail, not merely the number of clicks.

## 17. Required validation before Phase 2 sign-off

- Produce a first visual 101-territory map.
- Confirm every territory is selectable and recognisable.
- Confirm every land territory is reachable through a valid movement graph.
- Test island escape and invasion requirements.
- Review whether Russia and Türkiye work convincingly as theatre-edge powers.
- Confirm disputed-territory treatment and World State separation.
- Confirm the Rapid merge tree and Grand split tree are feasible.
- Run sample portal starts in at least ten strategically different locations.
- Review territory distribution against population, military capability and logistics.
- Complete a licensing ledger for every selected source.

## 18. Repository recommendation

Create the repository now. It should initially store design, research, source manifests and later prototypes. Creating it does not mean coding must start.

Suggested repository name: `future-conquest` as a neutral working title.

Recommended initial visibility: private during concept development. Before using GitHub Pages, either make the repository public or confirm that the selected GitHub plan supports Pages deployment from a private repository.

Proposed initial structure:

```text
README.md
docs/
  design/
    phase-01-game-identity.md
    phase-02-map-design.md
  research/
    sources.md
    decisions.md
  world-state/
    schema.md
data/
  source-manifests/
  authored/
assets/
  concept/
```

Raw third-party datasets should not be committed until their size, licence and update method have been reviewed. The repository should initially contain manifests and acquisition instructions rather than copied source archives.
