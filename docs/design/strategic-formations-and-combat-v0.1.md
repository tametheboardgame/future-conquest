# Strategic formations and combat resolution v0.1

## Design objective

Combat must communicate why the future army is terrifying without making it
invincible. Advanced infantry dominates unsupported modern troops, but massed
armour, artillery, air power, prepared terrain and repeated engagements damage
irreplaceable suits and eventually expose ordinary human bodies underneath.

The system resolves operational battles between map formations. It does not
simulate individual soldiers, weapon shots or tactical grid movement.

## Formation scale

The primary future formation is a task group of approximately 1,000 to 5,000
troops, with 2,500 as the default starting size. Small detachments perform raids,
reconnaissance, portal security and special objectives. Several task groups may
be placed under a future command so the player can issue one order without
losing the underlying formations.

Modern forces appear as battlegroups, brigades and regional or divisional
commands. Their displayed personnel includes combat support at an abstract
level. Police, security units and local garrisons use smaller formations.

The 50,000-troop Standard setting therefore begins with roughly twenty task
groups. Command containers, theatre orders and automation keep this manageable.

## Persistent formation state

Every formation tracks:

- active and wounded personnel;
- fully functional, damaged and broken powered armour;
- unarmoured future troops retaining energy rifles;
- readiness, cohesion, morale, fatigue and experience;
- supply state and remaining movement;
- stance, entrenchment and current orders;
- commander and general-command access;
- modern-force loyalty where applicable.

The player sees understandable bands and forecasts. Exact enemy statistics are
hidden unless intelligence quality is high.

## Powered-armour states

### Fully functional

Provides extreme protection from small arms, enhanced movement and the full
combat value of future sensors, strength and coordination. Heavy weapons can
still kill the wearer or damage the suit.

### Damaged

Some protection and powered functions remain, but mobility, sensors and strength
are unreliable. Damaged suits are substantially less effective against armour,
artillery and air-supported opponents.

### Broken or unarmoured

The soldier retains training, coordination and an energy rifle but is physically
vulnerable to modern weapons. These troops remain superior light infantry but
must not be used as if they were still super-soldiers.

## Modern formation roles

- Security and light infantry delay, observe and hold populated areas but are
  extremely vulnerable to intact future formations.
- Infantry uses terrain, fortification and numbers to impose delay and expose
  damaged future troops.
- Mechanised forces combine mobility, heavy weapons and protection. They are the
  first common modern formation capable of inflicting meaningful suit losses.
- Armoured forces create breakthrough and counterattack threats but depend on
  reconnaissance, infantry and logistics.
- Combined-arms commands coordinate infantry, armour, artillery, drones and air
  support. Concentrated combined arms can defeat an isolated future task group.
- Integrated local forces use the same military roles but add loyalty, political
  restrictions and possible refusal of orders.

## Player stances

- Assault: fastest and strongest attack, with substantially greater casualties,
  armour damage and civilian risk.
- Balanced: standard advance or defence.
- Cautious: reduced exposure and losses, but slower progress and greater chance
  that defenders withdraw successfully.
- Infiltrate: future-focused dispersed movement that reduces artillery and air
  exposure but is slow and less suitable for holding ground.
- Hold: prepared defence with no territorial advance.
- Disengage: prioritises breaking contact and preserving the formation.

Stance is a strategic instruction, not a tactical sequence the player must
micromanage.

## Daily battle sequence

### 1. Intelligence and contact

Detection, reconnaissance, deception and local support determine the accuracy
of the combat forecast and whether either side gains surprise. Neither side has
perfect information.

### 2. Interdiction and approach

Air power, artillery, drones, terrain and damaged crossings affect the attacker's
ability to reach the objective. Infiltration and dispersal reduce exposure but
slow concentration.

### 3. Main engagement

Combat power combines personnel, equipment type, powered-armour state,
readiness, cohesion, supply, stance, terrain and support. A limited random range
prevents perfect calculation without allowing arbitrary reversals of overwhelming
advantages.

### 4. Decision

The result is decisive attacker success, attacker success, stalemate, defender
success or decisive defender success. A successful attacker may advance towards
the strategic centre. A defender may hold, withdraw in order or rout.

### 5. Aftermath

The game applies casualties, wounded personnel, cohesion changes, armour damage,
equipment recovery, prisoners, civilian harm, infrastructure damage, escalation
and control delay. The player then chooses whether to continue, pause, withdraw
or bring in another formation on the following turn.

## Casualties and recovery

Combat casualties represent personnel removed from immediate action. After the
battle they divide into killed, critically wounded and recoverable wounded
according to weapon intensity, medical access, retreat conditions and possession
of the battlefield.

Future personnel never replenish naturally. Wounded troops may return after a
delay, but deaths and captures permanently reduce the army. Modern allied forces
may receive replacements only through their political authority and recruitment
system.

Possession of the battlefield matters. A victorious future force recovers broken
suits, components and energy weapons. A retreating formation abandons more
equipment and risks captured technology increasing global escalation.

## Armour damage and cannibalisation

Armour damage is tracked separately from casualties. A soldier may survive while
their suit moves from functional to damaged or from damaged to broken. Broken
suits enter a recoverable component pool when the battlefield is held.

Maintenance can restore damaged suits using time, supply and cannibalised parts.
It cannot manufacture replacement suits. Repairing one suit may therefore require
permanently stripping several others as the campaign progresses.

## Retreat and annihilation

A defeated formation attempts to retreat through a friendly, usable edge. Its
stance, cohesion, mobility, commander and enemy pursuit affect losses. If no
route exists, it may disperse, surrender or be destroyed.

Future troops are unlikely to surrender while organised, but isolated unarmoured
survivors can be captured. The general's formation follows special defeat and
rescue rules because capture can end the campaign.

## Territory resistance

Winning the field does not immediately secure a territory. Resistance bands are
minimal, organised, entrenched and national mobilisation. They modify enemy
combat strength and create an initial control delay of roughly one, three, six
or ten days before supply and occupation can stabilise.

Resistance may persist after organised military defeat. It is handled by
security, legitimacy and occupation systems rather than repeated identical
battles against invisible units.

## General and command

The general provides command access, improved coordination and more accurate
forecasts. Direct presence can improve cohesion and enable complex operations,
but risks the campaign-ending loss of the general.

The bonus should be meaningful but never so large that the optimal strategy is
to expose the general in every battle.

## Air and artillery

The future army begins with neither. Dispersal, concealment and powered armour
reduce their effects, but cannot eliminate them. Enemy air and artillery are
represented as support attached to a battle until separate air-control systems
are introduced.

Captured or allied modern aircraft and artillery may later provide support, but
require loyal crews, munitions, maintenance and secure bases. Capturing an airfield
or equipment does not automatically make it usable.

## Civilian and escalation consequences

High-intensity attacks in cities increase civilian casualties, infrastructure
damage and escalation. A cautious siege, negotiated surrender or infiltration
takes longer but may preserve both local cooperation and future armour.

Enemy use of heavy weapons in populated areas also has consequences. The system
must not assign every civilian casualty automatically to the player, but the
player remains responsible for choices that knowingly create disproportionate
risk.

## Automation

Players may delegate routine battles using rules such as avoid high-risk combat,
preserve fully functional armour, retreat below a cohesion threshold, or accept
casualties to seize the objective rapidly. Manual intervention selects stance,
reinforcement and withdrawal priorities rather than tactical unit placement.

## First balance result

The 14,000-engagement harness produces the intended initial relationships:

- 2,500 mostly armoured future troops decisively defeat 5,000 light security
  personnel with negligible casualties.
- The same future task group defeats a 5,000-person mechanised brigade but takes
  persistent armour damage.
- An 8,000-person combined-arms formation with air and artillery decisively stops
  an isolated 2,500-person cautious future attack.
- A task group with mostly damaged or broken armour loses to a supported
  mechanised brigade.
- Predominantly unarmoured future troops lose an urban attack against supported
  modern infantry.
- Modern allied formations remain useful conventional forces rather than
  automatic winners.

These are balancing relationships, not claims about real military performance.
