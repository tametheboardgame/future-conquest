# Phase 1: Game Identity and Vision

Status: Approved foundation  
Date: 1 August 2026

## Core premise

The game is a serious, single-player, turn-based strategy game set in present-day Europe. The player commands a finite army displaced from a future war. Its soldiers possess advanced powered armour and energy weapons, but have no reinforcements and cannot manufacture replacements for their most advanced technology.

The objective is to conquer and simultaneously control every required European territory before the modern world mobilises, adapts and overwhelms the invasion.

## Dynamic contemporary setting

Campaigns begin on the real-world date on which they are started. A versioned World State represents contemporary military capability, alliances, conflicts, deployments at an abstract regional level, political relationships and global tensions.

World States should be updated periodically rather than claim to reproduce live operational deployments. Every campaign records the World State version with which it began.

## Narrative

Centuries in the future, humanity is fighting a catastrophic war. Historical records are damaged, manipulated and contradictory. The surviving evidence suggests that the future enemy emerged from a social and political transformation that began somewhere in 21st-century Europe, but nobody can identify a single person, invention or event responsible.

A brilliant and controversial general concludes that surgical intervention is impossible. Their plan is to transport an enormous army into the 21st century, conquer Europe and deliberately redirect its development.

The general has studied naturally occurring temporal distortions but cannot control the portal's precise destination or date. The portal collapses much sooner than expected, allowing only a fraction of the intended force through. The surviving army is permanently isolated.

## Temporal revelation

If the player succeeds, it becomes increasingly clear that the invasion was always part of history. The conquest creates the political, technological and military conditions that produce the future war.

The broad temporal loop is fixed, but the player's conduct determines the form of the future:

- a restrained conquest may establish the faction the army originally served;
- a brutal conquest may create the enemy it travelled back to defeat;
- captured technology accelerates future weapons development;
- resistance and client governments evolve into later political factions;
- permanent emergency rule becomes the foundation of a future regime.

The game does not attempt to resolve conventional time-travel paradoxes. The invasion happened because it had already happened.

## Player identity

The player embodies the future general. The general exists as a physical command unit on the campaign map and provides strategic coordination, diplomatic authority and command bonuses. Keeping the general safe reduces immediate risk; moving them near the front improves operational effectiveness.

The general may be customised by name, appearance, gender and command specialisation.

## Future army

Campaign difficulty determines how many future soldiers pass through the portal:

- Campaign: 100,000
- Standard: 50,000
- Veteran: 25,000
- Desperate: 10,000

The exact values remain subject to simulation and balancing.

Future formations contain:

- fully armoured soldiers;
- soldiers with damaged or partially functional armour;
- unarmoured future soldiers retaining energy weapons.

Powered armour degrades through combat, movement, environmental exposure and insufficient maintenance. Destroyed suits can be cannibalised for components, creating a second permanent depletion curve alongside casualties.

## Conventional forces

Modern military and security forces may surrender, defect or join client governments. They can provide garrisons, logistics, engineering, armour, aircraft, naval transport and local knowledge. Their loyalty, supply requirements and political constraints prevent them from becoming uncomplicated reinforcements.

The campaign therefore evolves from a small, overwhelmingly advanced army into a larger but politically unstable coalition increasingly dependent on modern forces.

## Victory

Primary victory requires simultaneous control of every required European territory. Entering a territory is insufficient. Control requires the relevant administrative centre, removal or surrender of organised defenders, a functioning supply arrangement and an adequate garrison, allied authority or negotiated settlement.

## Defeat

The campaign ends if:

- the general is killed;
- the general is captured and cannot be recovered;
- the future army is destroyed;
- the player loses all viable territorial and military capacity;
- a strategic catastrophe makes continued conquest impossible.

A functionally defeated player should be permitted to continue for a final stand.

## Campaign format

One turn represents approximately one day.

- Rapid Campaign: about 45 to 60 territories and 4 to 8 hours.
- Standard Campaign: about 100 territories and 8 to 15 hours.
- Grand Campaign: about 170 to 220 territories and 15 to 30 hours.

These are design targets rather than promises and must be validated through playtesting.

## Strategic depth and automation

The player controls formations, logistics, diplomacy, occupation and strategic priorities without being forced to administer every settlement. Captured territories support automatic, guided and manual management.

Automation must be competent enough to remain viable. Manual control provides precision and modest efficiencies rather than being compulsory.

## Enemy model

The opposing side is entirely AI-controlled, but it does not begin as a single unified enemy. Countries react according to their capabilities, alliances, proximity, fear, political stability and assessment of the player's conduct. Coalitions and coordinated defence emerge as escalation rises.

Neither side receives perfect information.

## Global escalation

The world beyond Europe remains active. Escalation progresses from surveillance and sanctions through weapons shipments, advisers, expeditionary forces and ultimately nuclear authorisation.

Restraint, negotiated surrender and preservation of civilian life can delay escalation, but cannot make the world forget that the invasion occurred. Good conduct buys time rather than restoring normality.

## Tone

The tone is serious speculative military science fiction. Occasional dark humour may emerge through events and cultural misunderstandings, but the game treats conquest, civilian harm and occupation as consequential systems rather than decoration.

## Core identity

> A serious, turn-based strategy game in which a finite future army invades present-day Europe to change history. Every tactical victory consumes irreplaceable strength while the modern world mobilises, adapts and escalates. If the invasion succeeds, the player discovers that it created the future it was intended to prevent.
