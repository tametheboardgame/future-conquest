# Future Conquest visual design bible v1.0

Status: Approved visual canon

## Core proposition

Future Conquest uses painted military graphic-novel art with realistic human proportions. The future force is not today's military with larger armour and more lights. It is the result of roughly two centuries of military adaptation to sensor-saturated warfare, constrained energy, dense infrastructure, autonomous systems and collapsing logistics.

The central visual idea is an elite human operator inside a self-contained, modular combat platform. Humans are not biologically enhanced. Their armour augments strength, mobility, protection, endurance and battlefield awareness.

The technology must look functional, maintained and finite. Every component exists for a reason and every component can fail.

## Tone

- Dark, serious and oppressive
- Disciplined rather than theatrical
- Advanced but practical
- Capable despite exhaustion and scarcity
- Painterly rather than photorealistic
- Visually coherent with a serious European military graphic novel
- No uncomplicated heroic framing

## Design test

Every visual feature should answer these questions:

1. What battlefield problem does it solve?
2. Is the energy and maintenance burden justified?
3. Would a military trust it under failure conditions?
4. Can it be repaired, replaced or bypassed?
5. Does it remain useful when supply is poor?

Features that fail this test should be removed or moved to a specialist system.

## Powered infantry

### Operator

- Ordinary, unenhanced human
- Highly trained and physically capable
- Human face, proportions and limitations remain visible
- Helmet is standard but removable
- Helmet-off depictions are valid in secure or command contexts

### Silhouette

- Physically imposing but not gigantic
- Approximately 1.95 to 2.05 metres in armour for an average operator
- Broad enough to communicate powered strength, but agile through the waist, hips and limbs
- Shoulders and upper chest must not become oversized fantasy armour
- Flexible powered under-suit remains visible at joints and between hard modules
- No superhero stance, knight silhouette or walking-tank proportions

### Modular construction

Armour is designed for field replacement and cannibalisation. Damaged suits can be broken down to return other suits to service.

Standard modules include:

- Helmet and sensor module
- Left and right shoulder modules
- Chest protection and life-support module
- Back power, cooling and data module
- Upper-arm and forearm modules
- Glove modules
- Left and right thigh modules
- Knee modules
- Shin modules
- Boot and mobility modules
- Flexible under-suit sections

Modules use standard mechanical, power and data interfaces. Plates may have mismatched finishes, replacement fasteners, serials and repainting. Wear should communicate maintenance history, not merely surface scratches.

### Armour surface

The outer surface is a multifunctional smart skin, not ordinary painted plate and not a visible solar array.

Functions may include:

- Adaptive visible camouflage
- Near-infrared and thermal signature management
- Electromagnetic and radar-signature reduction
- Heat redistribution
- Passive sensing
- Environmental sealing
- Limited trickle energy harvesting
- Damage and diagnostic reporting

Adaptive camouflage is imperfect. It alters colour, pattern and spectral response, but does not provide full invisibility. It performs poorly when damaged, contaminated, overheated or moving rapidly.

### Protection

Protection is delivered through advanced materials and integrated systems rather than a visible bubble shield.

- Modular composite and ablative plates
- Flexible fragment-resistant under-layers
- Impact and shock attenuation
- Thermal dispersal
- Electromagnetic hardening
- Limited active countermeasures
- Localised reactive protection where justified

Small-arms resistance should look credible within the fiction, but armour is not invulnerable. Damage, degradation and loss of capability are core to the campaign.

### Helmet and optics

- Sealed helmet with removable or opening capability
- Narrow segmented optical system, not one uninterrupted glowing visor band
- Distinct optical elements and sensor apertures
- Minimal emission in normal use
- Cyan or pale blue light appears only when operationally useful or exposed by damage
- Integrated multispectral sensing, navigation, threat tagging and suit diagnostics

### Mobility

- Powered load bearing
- Enhanced sprinting and endurance
- Jump and obstacle assistance
- Climbing and breaching support
- Recoil and firing-position stabilisation
- No routine flight
- No universal individual tunnelling capability

Subsurface sensing, rapid entrenchment, wall breaching and burrowing engineering drones may exist as specialist capabilities.

## Weapons

### Primary weapon

The primary weapon remains separately carried because militaries value modularity, maintenance, replacement and loadout flexibility.

It interfaces deeply with the suit through:

- Targeting and sensor fusion
- Biometric and authority controls
- Recoil and stance compensation
- Power and diagnostic exchange
- Heat-status reporting
- Friend-or-foe safeguards

### Visual language

- Compact relative to the operator's enhanced strength
- Heavy, physical and industrial
- Protected replaceable power-cell housing
- Clear heat-management and service access
- Restrained geometry and attachments
- Minimal exposed glow
- Not a conventional rifle with decorative blue lighting
- Not a fantasy blaster or oversized multi-barrel prop

Integrated suit systems may include breaching tools, defensive countermeasures, micro-drone deployment or short-range emergency weapons. They do not replace the main rifle.

## The General

### Player identity

- The player selects a male-presenting or female-presenting General
- The player names the General
- Selected art and pronouns change accordingly
- Personality, authority and campaign capability do not change
- Narration can refer to the role as “the General” to remain compatible with player naming

### Character

- Older and visibly experienced
- Intelligent, controlled, calculating and precise
- Credible and persuasive
- Exhausted but formidable
- Potentially fanatical or dangerously certain, without obvious villain coding
- Their authority comes from competence and conviction, not ceremony

### Command armour

The General is always armoured.

- Command armour is derived from the same modular architecture as infantry armour
- Armour includes enhanced communications, command processing and battlefield awareness
- A long command overlayer or coat is permitted only as functional equipment
- The overlayer may provide weather protection, signature management, fragmentation protection, antenna integration or additional storage
- No capes, decorative medals, gold trim, aristocratic tailoring or ceremonial-only elements
- No exposed formal uniform replacing armour

Command systems may include:

- Tactical uplink
- Battlefield intelligence feed
- Friend-or-foe tracking
- Formation health and readiness monitoring
- Environmental and electromagnetic scan
- Encrypted communications
- Command relay and mesh-network support
- Redundant power, cooling and data routing

Male and female variants represent the same character role and must share equivalent armour architecture, authority and visual weight.

## Portal

The temporal portal is a violent spatial failure, not a manufactured doorway.

- Dark or visually impossible centre
- Pale cyan distortion at the outer boundary
- Violet-white energy appears during formation and instability
- Straight lines bend, duplicate or terminate incorrectly
- Rain, dust and debris are pulled inward before a pressure wave expels material
- Local lighting and perspective become geometrically wrong
- Avoid a clean lightning ring or fantasy magic-circle appearance

The portal is the principal use of violet-white energy in the visual language.

## Future world

- Decaying industrial science fiction with post-apocalyptic damage
- Europe remains inhabited, organised and militarised
- Infrastructure is repeatedly repaired instead of cleanly replaced
- Flooding, failed transport corridors, rationing, field fortification and improvised power systems are valid
- Historic and contemporary European architecture survives beside future systems
- Not every city is uniformly destroyed
- Show functioning refuges, workshops, command sites and emergency adaptation

The future should feel close to collapse, not empty after collapse.

## Present-day Europe

- Recognisable contemporary infrastructure, emergency services, civilian clothing and military equipment
- Ordinary and comparatively warm before the arrival
- Present-day forces respond rationally to an unknown armed incursion
- They must not be visually coded as villains
- Locations should remain generic unless a scene explicitly requires a real place
- Do not commit the introduction to one portal location because campaign placement can vary

## Palette and light roles

The palette is role-based rather than decorative.

- Near black: deep structure, negative space and interface background
- Charcoal: armour base and industrial surfaces
- Cold blue-grey: secondary armour, atmosphere and future infrastructure
- Dirty off-white: practical light, ash, labels and worn markings
- Muted earth tones: adaptive camouflage and present-day materials
- Restrained cyan: future sensors, selected interface states and exposed active systems
- Violet-white: portal formation and temporal instability only
- Warm sodium and neutral daylight: present-day civilian contrast

Avoid broad neon accents. Technology should usually be dark when functioning normally.

Suggested digital anchors:

- Void: `#03070B`
- Command black: `#071018`
- Armour charcoal: `#151D23`
- Steel blue-grey: `#26333D`
- Worn metal: `#58636A`
- Dirty light: `#D7E2E7`
- Sensor cyan: `#74DCEA`
- Portal pale: `#D8FAFF`
- Portal violet: `#7964D8`
- Present sodium: `#B69A72`

These are guidance anchors, not mandatory flat fills in painted art.

## Insignia and markings

No current generated emblem is automatically canon.

Future faction identity should eventually use:

- A fractured circle or incomplete timeline
- A breach, branch or directional break
- Simple geometry readable at small sizes
- No eagles, wings, skulls, laurels or national heraldry

Until a final insignia is approved, artwork should use neutral serials, unit bars and minimal non-specific markings.

## Game pieces and interface translation

- Motion comic and event art use painted compositions
- Portraits use the same painterly material and lighting language
- Unit pieces use simplified armoured silhouettes rather than miniature full paintings
- Tactical icons should derive from module shapes and the eventual insignia
- Cyan represents future-force systems, focus and selection
- Violet-white is reserved for portal and temporal states
- Present-day forces use contemporary colours and restrained military symbology
- Avoid adding visual detail that becomes noise at game-map scale

## Prohibited drift

Do not introduce:

- Biological super-soldier anatomy
- Giant fantasy armour or exaggerated shoulders
- Pristine parade equipment
- Full active invisibility as a normal state
- Constant bright armour lighting
- Universal visible force-field bubbles
- Integrated primary guns replacing all modular weapons
- Personal flight as standard infantry mobility
- Individual high-speed tunnelling as standard infantry mobility
- Gothic, imperial or aristocratic command styling
- Decorative capes, wings, skulls or excessive medals
- Space-opera vehicles or alien visual language
- Present-day defenders framed as inherently evil
- Visual confirmation of the unknown catalyst
